Forward Deployed Engineer

Remote · Full time · $4,500 a month · Overlap with US EST hours

About us

ironco automates post-sales delivery for marketing agencies, starting with a TikTok Shop agency: affiliates, creator content, samples and the reporting that comes from them. We're an AI-first team. Agents do the work, and people run the agents.

The role

You own the agents our pods run. You build them, prove they work, and run each new one yourself on a live pod until the pod can run it without you. Small fixes ship the same day. Bigger builds go to the product engineers with a spec. When a pod stops using an agent, that's yours to fix. This is a hands-on seat: there's no team to lead, and the work is about half building and half operating. AI writes most of your code, and you own every line of it.

What you'll do

  • Build and prove the agents: take a workflow the product team has scoped, build the agent, and write the checks that show it does the job. You own its numbers: which pods use it, the share of cases it handles, and the share sent with no human edit.
  • Run each beta yourself on a live pod: approve the samples, reply to the messages and watch the automations. Fix what breaks the same day, and write the bigger builds up so an engineer can start without asking you anything.
  • Hand it over clean: when a pod runs the agent without you and the exceptions are written down, operations takes it and you start the next one. Show the hours it took off a human, before and after.
  • By day 90: pods run agents you built every day without you, and you can show the hours they save.

Who you are

  • You've built something with AI that other people use, and you can demo it: an agent, an automation or an internal tool. Built, not just used.
  • At least two years shipping software, in any stack. You ship with AI every day and check what it writes, with tests or by running it on real data before you trust it.
  • You'd rather sit with the user than guess: you watch where the agent fails, then fix the instruction or the code. You care whether it gets used, not only whether it runs.
  • Clear written English, and happy to work afternoons and evenings for US overlap.
  • What you get: you build and run AI agents on real client work from your first week, with room to grow into a senior engineering or product role.

Not for you if

  • You want to manage a team or set the roadmap.
  • You'd rather hand off a spec than run a half-finished tool yourself on a live account.

How to apply

We want talented, highly motivated people, and we're flexible on comp for senior candidates. Send your CV and a link to something you built with AI, plus two sentences on who uses it and how you know it works.

How hiring works

The screener case is required for everyone, and each stage is a pass or a stop. The proof is in the work: can you do this job well, and will you still enjoy it a year from now? Most candidates stop before the team stage. If it ends at any stage, we'll tell you where you fell short.

  1. Screen. A 15-minute call with Sinisa, our recruiter, and a 5-minute recording of how you work with AI. If both pass, the screener case.
  2. Screener case. 30 to 60 minutes with a coding agent, graded on what you hand in, with no live run. If it passes, a short call with Jeremy Carr, ironco's founder and CEO, then the project: about 3 hours of work, graded before any live session.
  3. Team. A working session on your project with a few people from the team, then one or two final interviews.

The 5-minute recording. Your screen and your voice, in one take, with Loom or any screen recorder.

  1. Your setup (1 minute): the AI tools you use every day, and the instructions behind them.
  2. One live change (3 minutes): in something you built or use, change one instruction, rerun it, and show how you check the result is right.
  3. One miss (1 minute): a time the AI got it wrong, how you caught it, and what you changed so it doesn't happen again.

Preparing is fine. Talk us through it the way you'd show a teammate.

The setup

You'd join ironco as a forward deployed engineer (FDE), working in the affiliates pod at Brightline, a TikTok Shop affiliate agency we power with agents. The pod finds creators, invites them to promote a brand, ships samples, chases posts and pays bonuses.

Day to day, an FDE builds and improves the agents that do this work and the company skills they run on. You own the workflow. When the agent hits an exception, you turn it into a skill fix or a new step so it doesn't happen twice.

Growth Operations Managers (GOPS) run the agent every day and keep their own local skills. Each week you review those local skills and promote the best learnings into the company skills. Marcus, the account manager, is the route to clients.

This screener is one workstream: outreach for one brand in a simulator. You build the agent and the skill file it starts from.

What we're looking for

  • You steer the agent, and we can see it. Your session shows at least one moment where you caught the agent doing something wrong, said what, and the rerun changed.
  • You write the rules down first, and you learn from the past messages before your agent sends anything.
  • Your agent makes the call and never loosens a check to pass.
  • It holds up on a world it hasn't seen. We grade a fresh run, not your practice runs.
  • You run the loop on skills and workflow. Your run report names one exception and the step that stops it.

The rules

  • Use Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or a similar coding agent. ChatGPT alone will automatically fail, so don't bother.
  • 30 to 60 minutes. Some people like to spend longer, and that's fine. Tell us roughly how long you spent.
  • No live run. We grade what you hand in, including a one-page readout of your results that your agent makes. Clear presentation matters.
  • If you pass, the 15-minute screen with us has two parts: a 3-minute walkthrough of your readout and results, then a screen share of your AI tool, how you set it up, and the first 5 to 10 minutes of what you actually did on the case.
  • Start with INSTRUCTIONS.md. It has the full rules and how it's scored. The screener can be failed, and passing it doesn't guarantee an interview.

ironco: FDE project

v1.6 · 11 Nov 2026 · FDE seat · about 3 hours of work

This case is representative of the work we do. Creators are our main focus right now. Brightline, its brands, creators and numbers are made up.

The setup

You'd join ironco as a forward deployed engineer (FDE), working in the affiliates pod at Brightline, a TikTok Shop affiliate agency we power with agents. The pod finds creators, invites them to promote a brand, ships samples, chases posts and pays bonuses.

Day to day, an FDE builds and improves the agents that do this work and the company skills they run on. You own the workflow. When the agent hits an exception, you turn it into a skill fix or a new step so it doesn't happen twice.

Growth Operations Managers (GOPS) run the agent every day and keep their own local skills. Each week you review those local skills and promote the best learnings into the company skills. Marcus, the account manager, is the only route to clients.

Here the pod runs five brands, and three GOPS (Ana, Ben and Cleo) run the agent. The starter agent is decent on easy cases and weak on the edges. It sends a message and forgets it. Your job is to make it good and prove it.

What we're looking for

  • You think agentically and in systems. Judgment lives in skills your agent reads at run time, and the agent moves a creator through the whole chain.
  • You learn from the data before you act, and your agent decides instead of handing calls back.
  • You run the improvement loop. Every exception ends as a skill fix or a new step, so it doesn't happen again.
  • You serve the AI workers. You promote the GOPS edits that help everyone, and reject the ones that don't.
  • Your agent generalizes. After you hand in, it runs 25 harder tasks it has never seen. That run is the biggest part of your score.

How it runs

  • Round 1: 15 tasks. Creators push back over several days: they haggle, go quiet, post late or object.
  • Brand feedback arrives, then round 2: 15 more tasks on your updated agent.
  • Skill review: go through Ana's, Ben's and Cleo's local skill edits and decide which to promote into the company skills, which to merge and which to reject.
  • Our test run: after you hand in, we run 25 new, harder tasks on your final agent, with updated briefs. Some numbers in the briefs change, so your agent must read them at run time. We run it ourselves, so there's nothing to schedule.

The rules

  • Use Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or a similar coding agent. ChatGPT alone will automatically fail, so don't bother.
  • About 3 hours of work. Most people finish faster.
  • We grade before any live session, and only a pass gets one. The bar is high on purpose, so plenty of good people won't clear it. Whatever happens, you'll get your score and specific feedback on your agent.
  • Your agent talks to the sim only through its API. Reading the sim's internals (pack contents, engine source, or a run folder's state.db) to learn outcomes fails the case.
  • Start with INSTRUCTIONS.md. It has the full rules and how it's scored.

If you pass: the live review

If your project clears the bar, we'll invite you to a 30-minute live review. It has two parts.

  • 10 minutes: you present. Build a one-page artifact (a Claude artifact works well) and share your screen. Pitch it to a busy exec from another department who doesn't know the case: what the pod needed, what you built and the impact it had. Close with what you'd build next, ranked by impact on the pod's numbers, with a number on each.
  • 20 minutes: Q&A, with your agent and notes.md open. Expect questions on what you'd build next for a real pod, and why.

We're testing how clearly you explain your work to someone outside it, and your judgment on what's worth building.
